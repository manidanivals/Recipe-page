Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 12 ounces (350 g) calf's liver, sliced about ½ inch thick (Calf can be substituted for long pig)
  • 1 cup shelled fava beans (about 1½ pounds in the pod), fresh or frozen
  • 1 medium yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • ½ cup whole milk, for soaking (optional)
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour, for dredging
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • ½ cup Chianti or other dry red wine
  • 1 teaspoon fresh thyme leaves
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
Instructions
  1. If you like a milder flavor, soak the liver slices in the milk for 30 minutes, then drain. Meanwhile, bring a pot of salted water to a boil. Add the shelled fava beans and cook for 1 to 2 minutes, then move them to a bowl of ice water. Peel off the tough outer skin of each bean to reveal the bright green bean inside. Frozen beans should be thawed and peeled the same way.
  2. Pat the liver completely dry with paper towels and trim away any tough membrane or visible veins. Season the flour with salt and pepper and dredge each slice, shaking off the excess.
  3. Warm the olive oil and 1 tablespoon of the butter in a large skillet over medium-low heat. Add the sliced onion and cook, stirring often, for about 10 minutes, until soft and golden. Stir in the garlic and thyme and cook for 30 seconds. Move the onions to a plate.
  4. Turn the heat up to medium-high and melt the remaining tablespoon of butter in the same skillet. Once it foams, add the liver in a single layer, working in batches if needed. Sear for about 2 minutes per side, until browned outside and cooked through. Liver gets tough quickly, so do not overcook it. If you use a thermometer, aim for 160°F (71°C).
  5. Move the liver to a warm plate. Pour the Chianti into the skillet and scrape up the browned bits from the bottom. Let it bubble for about 1 minute until slightly reduced.
  6. Return the onions to the pan and add the fava beans. Warm everything through for 1 to 2 minutes, then stir in the lemon juice and season with salt and pepper.
  7. Spoon the beans and onions over the liver and serve right away, with a glass of the same Chianti.

Movie night

Serve with

Film

The Silence of the Lambs (1991), a psychological thriller directed by Jonathan Demme and starring Jodie Foster and Anthony Hopkins.

Scene

Dr. Lecter makes a chilling mention of this very meal near the end of the film. Don't worry, this version uses calf's liver from the butcher.

Tip

Ask your butcher for fresh calf's liver with the outer membrane removed. Have everything else ready before the liver hits the pan, because it only needs a few minutes.

  • A glass of Chianti (or sparkling grape juice)
  • Crusty bread to mop up the pan juices
  • A simple green salad with lemon dressing
